# EDGE MAP

## Purpose

This document defines possible sources of trading edge.

An edge is a repeatable relationship that may create
positive expected value after realistic costs and risk.

---

## 1. Market Edges

- Momentum
- Mean reversion
- Trend following
- Breakouts
- Volatility
- Volume behavior
- Liquidity
- Market microstructure
- Relative strength
- Cross-sectional relationships

---

## 2. Information Edges

- Earnings
- Corporate announcements
- Economic releases
- News
- Sector developments
- Analyst revisions
- Market expectations

---

## 3. Statistical Edges

- Autocorrelation
- Mean reversion
- Volatility clustering
- Conditional relationships
- Cross-sectional relationships
- Regime persistence

---

## 4. Behavioral Edges

- Overreaction
- Underreaction
- Herding
- Momentum behavior
- Panic selling
- FOMO-driven behavior

---

## 5. Execution Edges

- Spread
- Slippage
- Liquidity
- Order timing
- Market impact
- Execution latency

---

## 6. Machine Learning Edges

- Feature relationships
- Nonlinear relationships
- Regime classification
- Probability estimation
- Anomaly detection

---

## 7. AI/LLM Contributions

LLMs should primarily assist with:

- research
- hypothesis generation
- information synthesis
- reasoning
- adversarial analysis
- experiment design
- explanation

LLMs must not be treated as an independent source
of guaranteed trading edge.

---

## Edge Validation

Every claimed edge must answer:

1. What is the mechanism?
2. Why should it exist?
3. What data supports it?
4. How many observations exist?
5. Does it survive out-of-sample testing?
6. Does it survive transaction costs?
7. Does it survive slippage?
8. Does it survive parameter changes?
9. Does it survive different market regimes?
10. What evidence would falsify it?
